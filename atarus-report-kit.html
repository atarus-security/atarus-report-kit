Executive Summary

Write for a non-technical reader. The CEO, the board, the client's legal team.

Good exec summaries answer three questions:
1. What did we do? (scope + dates)
2. What did we find? (most important findings, in plain English)
3. What should the business do? (top priorities)

Avoid jargon. If you say "XSS" explain it as "a vulnerability that can steal user sessions". Keep it under 300 words.
High-level program recommendations beyond the individual findings. Think: what should this company's security program look like in 6 months?

Findings

The meat of the report. Each finding teaches the client something about their security posture.

Methodology

Describe your testing approach. This shows the client you followed a rigorous process.

Common frameworks to reference: PTES, OWASP Testing Guide, NIST SP 800-115, OSSTMM.

Typical phases to describe: Reconnaissance, Scanning/Enumeration, Vulnerability Analysis, Exploitation, Post-Exploitation, Reporting.

Conclusion

Wrap it up. What's the overall security posture, and what should happen next?

A good conclusion includes:
1. Overall posture assessment (strong, adequate, concerning, critical)
2. Key risk themes across findings
3. Suggested next steps (remediation, retest, training)

Keep it 2-3 paragraphs. This is the last thing the executive reader sees.

Disclaimer
